How to read the table

The ranges in the table are the time of the operation itself. The total time spent at the clinic is longer, because preparation and anesthesia come before and time in the recovery room comes after. Even under the same name, an operation moves up or down within the range depending on whether one area or several are treated.

What these figures do not mean

These figures are not any particular clinic's average; they are the general ranges the UK NHS publishes. How many hours your own operation will take is settled at consultation, based on the planned scope.

What makes the difference?

  • Extent of the area treated — Whether one area or several are treated changes the time considerably.
  • Whether skin is removed — If loose skin is excised, the time for planning the incision and closing it is added.
  • Type of anesthesia — The wider the scope and the longer the operation, the more often general anesthesia is chosen.

Why lift procedures take the longest

Lift procedures run in the order of incision planning → tissue removal → fixing the remaining tissue → closure. How much is removed depends on how much skin has loosened, and how the remaining tissue is fixed also has to be decided, so there are many steps. The UK NHS gives a tummy tuck as 2 to 5 hours for exactly this breadth. If the navel is repositioned, that adds time as well.

For liposuction, the number of areas sets the time

Liposuction runs in the order of marking → anesthesia → fluid infusion → suction → finishing. Each step is repeated for every area treated, so whether one area or three or four are treated determines the total. Movement within the NHS range of 1 to 3 hours is mostly this difference. Marking the areas to be treated, done standing, happens before the operation.

Why implant surgery is relatively consistent

Surgery using implants is planned by combining three things: incision site, the layer the implant is placed in, and the type of implant. Once those three are decided at consultation the operation itself is fairly consistent, and the UK NHS gives 60 to 90 minutes. If sagging is present and a lift is done at the same time, the steps of a lift procedure are added and the time goes beyond this range.

What to confirm at consultation

  • Where your planned operation falls within the ranges above
  • Whether a separate anesthesia provider is present

Asking about your own case

The table above gives general ranges and does not apply to you as it stands. Rephrasing the question like this at consultation gets you an answer for your own case.

Where does the scope I'm having fall in the table? How many hours will I be at the clinic in total?

Common misunderstandings

Does a longer operation mean a bigger operation?

Generally it takes longer when the area treated is larger, but time alone is a poor measure of size, because the same operation differs depending on whether one area or several are treated. It is more accurate to ask how many hours your own planned scope will take.

If I have several procedures at once, does the time add up?

It does not add up exactly, but the total is longer. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons states that the longer the operation, the higher the chance of a blood clot, so deciding how much to do in one session is itself a safety matter.
