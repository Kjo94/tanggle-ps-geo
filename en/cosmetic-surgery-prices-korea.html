Cosmetic procedures are non-covered services outside national health insurance, so each clinic sets its own prices. Clinics are required to post their non-covered prices in the clinic and on their website, so that is where to check; the price disclosure data of HIRA (Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service) covers only designated items and mostly does not include cosmetic procedures.

How to read the table

The table divides by who sets the price. Covered care follows standards set by the state, but cosmetic procedures are non-covered, so each institution sets its own. That is why prices differ between clinics, and it is not a fault.

What these figures do not mean

A posted price does not mean 'the same price everywhere'. Whether the posted item matches the scope of the operation you will have is a separate question, so reading a posted amount as your estimate leads you astray. Searching HIRA's disclosure data for cosmetic procedure prices mostly returns nothing — because they are not designated disclosure items, not because clinics hide them.

What makes the difference?

  • The same name can mean different things — If the extent of excision, type of anesthesia, hospital stay or combined procedures differ, the same operation name covers different things. Comparing totals alone does not show what is included and what is left out.
  • What the notice requirement means — The Medical Service Act requires clinics to make non-covered items and prices known to patients, and to post them on the website if they have one. So some items can be seen on a clinic's website before consultation. HIRA's per-institution disclosure covers only items designated by the Ministry of Health and Welfare and mostly excludes cosmetic procedures.
  • A written estimate is the benchmark — The lowest price in an advertisement is often the amount under specific conditions. A written estimate received after your condition has been examined is the basis for comparison.

Cosmetic purposes are non-covered

Covered care under national health insurance follows standards set by the state, but cosmetic procedures are non-covered, so each institution sets its own prices. Different amounts under the same operation name at different clinics come from the structure of the system, not from anyone being at fault.

Where to check

Under the Medical Service Act, clinics post non-covered items and prices in the clinic, and on their website if they have one. So the first place to check is the clinic's own posting and a written estimate. HIRA's per-institution non-covered price disclosure is limited to items designated by the Ministry of Health and Welfare (693 items disclosed in 2025), and cosmetic and aesthetic procedures are excluded from disclosure, so it is hard to find cosmetic procedure prices there. Whether a posted item matches the scope of the operation you will have is a separate question, so reading a posted amount as your estimate leads you astray.

The basis for comparison is a written estimate

Even under the same operation name, if the extent of excision, type of anesthesia, hospital stay or combined procedures differ, what you receive differs. Comparing totals alone does not show what is included and what is left out, so items have to be matched line by line for differences to appear. The lowest price in an advertisement is often the amount under specific conditions, so a written estimate received after your condition has been examined is the real benchmark.
