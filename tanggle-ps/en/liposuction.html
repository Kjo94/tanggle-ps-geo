What is specific to Liposuction?

Liposuction removes excess fat from specific areas; both the American Society of Plastic Surgeons and the NHS state that it is not a treatment for obesity or a substitute for diet and exercise, and does not remove cellulite or stretch marks. Where skin has already stretched, reducing fat leaves the skin behind, so skin removal may be considered alongside.

How is Liposuction performed?

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons defines liposuction (lipoplasty) as a surgical procedure that removes excess fat from specific areas of the body — the abdomen and waist, back, calves and ankles, chest, face (cheeks, chin, neck), hips and buttocks, inner knee, thighs and upper arms. It can be performed alone or together with a tummy tuck, breast reduction or facelift.

The steps, as the society describes them:

  • Anesthesia — intravenous sedation or general anesthesia. The NHS notes it is usually general anesthesia, sometimes an epidural.
  • Small incisions and infusion — through small, inconspicuous incisions, diluted local anesthetic is infused to reduce bleeding and trauma; the NHS describes this solution as reducing blood loss, bruising and swelling.
  • Cannula and suction — a thin hollow tube is moved back and forth to loosen fat, which is then suctioned out with a surgical vacuum or syringe.
  • Techniques — traditional, power-assisted (vibrating cannula), ultrasound-assisted (UAL) and its newer generation VASER, laser-assisted and water-assisted. The technique is one line of the quote.
  • Duration and stay — the NHS says it usually takes 1 to 3 hours and most people stay in hospital overnight; smaller procedures may be same-day, so ask.

Results appear once swelling and fluid retention subside. The society states the fat removed stays off with continued diet and fitness, but substantial weight gain can alter the result.

So four things to confirm at consultation: which areas and how much, which technique, which anesthesia, and same-day or overnight. If these differ, the procedure and the price differ even under the same name.

How do you prepare for Liposuction?

The first step is confirming you are a suitable candidate. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons lists adults within 30% of their ideal weight with firm, elastic skin and good muscle tone, healthy individuals without conditions that impair healing, and non-smokers and non-vapers.

What the procedure does not do is worth knowing in advance.

  • It is not a treatment for obesity or a substitute for diet and exercise — the society and the NHS use the same sentence.
  • It is not an effective treatment for cellulite, and the NHS adds that it will not remove stretch marks.
  • Where skin is soft and thin from stretch marks, weight loss or aging, results may be suboptimal and a separate skin-removal procedure may be needed, the society explains.

The rest of the preparation is the same as for other surgery — disclose conditions, medications and supplements, adjust smoking and alcohol, and complete pre-anesthesia tests. Specific to liposuction: a compression garment is worn constantly for several weeks afterwards (NHS), so plan your schedule and clothing accordingly.

What does recovery after Liposuction involve?

Right after surgery, dressings are applied to the incisions and an elastic bandage, support bra or compression garment is worn to minimize swelling. If drains are placed you may be asked to record how much fluid collects (American Society of Plastic Surgeons). The NHS says the garment is worn constantly for several weeks.

The week-by-week course, per the society's recovery guidance:

  • Week 1 — pain, swelling and bruising; medication may be needed; activity is significantly limited.
  • Weeks 2–3 — you feel better and may return to work depending on the job; exercise stays limited.
  • Weeks 4–5 — bruising should disappear and swelling starts to reduce.
  • Week 6 onward — swelling keeps improving; activity and gentle exercise increase gradually.

The NHS is somewhat more conservative — gentle exercise at around 3 to 4 weeks, strenuous activity avoided for 10 to 12 weeks, bruising and swelling for up to 6 months, and numbness resolving in 6 to 8 weeks. Fluid from the cuts and swollen ankles are also listed as expected.

Return to work and driving depend on your job and the extent of surgery; knowing these ranges lets you ask about your own timeline at consultation.

When should you contact the clinic after Liposuction?

Most swelling and bruising settles over time. The following, however, are described as signs to contact the clinic straight away rather than waiting for a scheduled follow-up.

  • Fever — may indicate infection.
  • Pain that improves and then worsens again
  • Sudden swelling, especially on one side only
  • Bleeding that does not stop, or a wound that opens
  • Pain and swelling in one calf — the American Society of Plastic Surgeons lists deep vein thrombosis (DVT) among the risks of this group of operations.
  • Shortness of breath or chest pain — seek emergency care immediately.

ASPS notes that limited movement during recovery raises the risk of clots, so light walking is sometimes encouraged early. Ask the operating surgeon what is allowed and from when.

Ask for out-of-hours and holiday contact details before the operation. Emergency arrangements are something to confirm at consultation.

What are the risks of Liposuction?

Every operation carries risk. The following summarises risks published by bodies such as the American Society of Plastic Surgeons and Mayo Clinic. It does not predict the outcome for any particular clinic or individual. The risks that apply to you must be explained to you directly at consultation.

  • Anesthesia risks — reactions vary with the type of anesthesia used
  • Deep vein thrombosis (DVT), cardiac and pulmonary complications — a clot forming in a deep leg vein can travel to the lungs and become dangerous. ASPS lists this explicitly; the likelihood rises with longer operating times and reduced movement during recovery. Ask at consultation how your risk is assessed and what prevention is used — compression devices, early walking and, where indicated, medication.
  • Bleeding or hematoma — blood collecting at the surgical site
  • Infection — at the wound site
  • Poor wound healing — recovery slower than expected
  • Fat necrosis — death of some fat tissue
  • Skin loss or skin necrosis — where blood supply to the incision is insufficient
  • Persistent pain — pain that lasts longer than expected
  • Unfavorable scarring — any incision leaves a scar; the degree varies between individuals
  • Asymmetry or a suboptimal aesthetic result — further surgery may be needed to correct it
  • Numbness or changes in skin sensation — temporary or lasting
  • Fluid accumulation (seroma) — drainage or further treatment may be required
  • Skin discoloration and prolonged swelling — lasting longer than expected
  • Recurrent skin looseness — skin can loosen again over time
  • Possibility of revisional surgery — further surgery may be needed for correction

What most affects risk is the surgeon's qualification and experience, the facility, the anesthesia staff, and the arrangements for emergencies. Verifying these four at consultation is the most practical safety check available to a patient.

Ask directly how clot prevention is handled. Smoking, obesity, a history of clots and hormone medication are known to raise the risk — disclose them if they apply to you. Long-haul flights are relevant here: if you are travelling for treatment, ask how soon after surgery it is safe to fly.

During recovery, fever, worsening pain, sudden swelling, bleeding or wound separation — or pain and swelling in one calf, breathlessness or chest pain — should never be self-assessed. Contact the treating clinic and seek emergency care immediately. Confirm the contact route and hours before surgery, and if you are returning to another country, agree how follow-up will be handled.

What determines the cost of Liposuction in Seoul?

What sets the price of liposuction is the number and size of areas, the technique (traditional, power-assisted, ultrasound/VASER, laser, water-assisted), the anesthesia (sedation or general), same-day discharge or overnight stay, whether garments and follow-up are included, and whether it is combined with a tummy tuck or other surgery. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons says the total is made up of anesthesia, facility, tests, garments, prescriptions and the surgeon's fee, which depends on experience, technique and location.

Under the same name, one area or three, a cannula-only technique or ultrasound change the operating time and anesthesia load — and that is the price difference. Totals alone hide what is included, so ask each clinic the same six items in the same order: areas, technique, anesthesia, stay, garments, combined surgery.

Ask for a quote broken down by area so it can be compared like for like. This site does not publish the listed clinic's prices; ranges published by bodies in other countries are on the guide page about non-covered pricing.

How do you verify a surgeon in Korea?

In Korea liposuction can be performed by any licensed physician regardless of specialty, so board certification in plastic surgery has to be checked separately. Look the surgeon up by name in the Korean Society of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgeons' specialist search, and check the clinic's number of specialists by field and registered operating rooms in the HIRA hospital registry.

Questions specific to liposuction:

  • Technique and device — traditional, power-assisted, ultrasound (VASER), laser or water-assisted, and why that choice.
  • Areas and volume — which areas and how much; larger volumes raise anesthesia and safety demands.
  • Anesthesia and who provides it — sedation or general, and whether a dedicated anesthesia provider is present.
  • Skin assessment — how they decide whether fat removal alone is enough or skin removal is needed.
  • Consultation–surgery match — whether the doctor you consult is the one who operates, and whether the consent form names the same surgeon.

The NHS advises checking the surgeon's registration and also their complication history and post-operative follow-up; in Korea the society searches and the HIRA registry serve that role.
