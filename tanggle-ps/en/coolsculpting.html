Recovery after CoolSculpting (Cryolipolysis) varies with the extent of the procedure and the patient's condition; the clinic provides an individual schedule at consultation.

How does CoolSculpting (Cryolipolysis) differ from other options?

CoolSculpting (Cryolipolysis) differs from other options in approach and recovery, so the choice is made at consultation based on skin and tissue condition.

What is specific to CoolSculpting (Cryolipolysis)?

CoolSculpting works by cooling fat cells, with no incision and no anaesthesia. It reduces fat in a treated area but does not tighten loose skin and is not a substitute for weight loss. The change is smaller in scale than surgery and results appear gradually, so the first question is whether your case falls within what this method can address.

How do you prepare for CoolSculpting (Cryolipolysis)?

Preparation differs greatly depending on the method used, so the starting point is to identify exactly which procedure you will receive during the consultation.

  • Medications and supplements — some can affect bruising and bleeding, so list prescription drugs, supplements and herbal remedies.
  • Medical history and allergies — previous reactions matter, particularly for procedures that use injectable products.
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding — some methods are postponed.
  • Products and devices — ask for the product name, whether it is approved in Korea, and whether it is being used within the approved indication.
  • Who performs it — confirm that a doctor performs the procedure.
  • Scheduling — swelling or bruising can remain, so avoid the days before an important event.

Ask how long the result lasts and how often it must be repeated. That determines both the schedule and the real total cost.

What are the risks of CoolSculpting (Cryolipolysis)?

CoolSculpting works by cooling fat cells — there is no incision and no anesthesia. The risks of surgery (anesthesia, clots, scarring) therefore do not apply, but this method has risks of its own.

  • Redness, swelling and bruising — common immediately after treatment and usually temporary.
  • Pain, stinging or tingling — during treatment and for some days afterwards.
  • Reduced sensation — the area may feel numb for a time and usually recovers.
  • Frostbite or cold-related skin injury — rare but recognised.
  • Paradoxical adipose hyperplasia (PAH) — a rare reaction in which fat in the treated area does not shrink but instead becomes larger and firmer. It does not resolve on its own and may require a separate corrective procedure. Ask whether the clinic is familiar with it and how they respond if it occurs.
  • Limited effect — it does not tighten loose skin and is not a substitute for weight loss. If skin laxity is also present, this method alone will not address it.

The first thing to establish is whether this is the right method for you. Where skin is loose or the volume of fat is large, another approach may be needed, so comparing it against other treatments for the same area is the accurate way to decide.

Contact the treating clinic if the area remains firm and enlarged months afterwards, or if you develop severe pain, changes in skin colour, or blistering.

What determines the cost of CoolSculpting (Cryolipolysis) in Seoul?

For non-surgical treatments the price is set by what is used, how much, and how many times — not by a single figure. Comparing totals alone compares quotes that are not equivalent.

  • Product and quantity — ask for the product name and the unit (volume or number of units).
  • Number of areas treated.
  • Number of sessions — whether the quoted price is for one session or the full course.
  • Maintenance — how long the effect lasts and the cost of repeating it.
  • Additional items — topical or sedation options, aftercare and medication, included or not.
  • Correction policy — the conditions and cost if the result is not as expected.

Cosmetic treatments are not covered by Korean national health insurance. Clinics set their own prices for non-covered items and are required to publish them, which is why prices differ between clinics.

Who performs CoolSculpting (Cryolipolysis), and how do you verify them?

Even without surgery, who performs the treatment determines both the result and your safety. In Korea, medical procedures may only be performed by licensed medical practitioners — confirm that a doctor performs it.

  • Who performs it — whether the doctor you consulted does it personally.
  • Specialist qualification — 'plastic surgery specialist' and 'doctor practising plastic surgery' are different. The Korean Society of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgeons lets you verify this directly.
  • Clinic registration — registration details are held by the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service.
  • Products used — approved in Korea, and used within the approved indication.
  • Response to complications — what the clinic can manage on site and how to reach them.

Review scores are a weak guide because they are not a measure of safety. Verifying registered qualifications and product information is more reliable.
