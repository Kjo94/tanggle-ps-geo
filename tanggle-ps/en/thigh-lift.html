How does Thigh Lift differ from other lift procedures?

A thigh lift uses an incision following the groin crease, with an incision descending along the inner thigh where laxity extends further down. The area is under constant tension while walking and sits against underwear and clothing, so early wound care and maintained compression affect the result. Which incision is needed depends on the extent of laxity and requires direct assessment.

How is Thigh Lift performed?

A thigh lift (thighplasty) reduces excess skin and fat to smooth the contours of the thighs. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons describes incisions for the inner (medial) and outer (lateral) thigh, and Cleveland Clinic adds a mini lift (a small groin incision) and a spiral lift (from the buttock to the groin) — four types. The type is what determines where the scar sits and how long it is.

The steps, as the society describes them:

  • Anesthesia — intravenous sedation or general anesthesia. Cleveland Clinic performs it under general anesthesia and says it may take about two hours.
  • Inner thigh incision — either in the groin, extending downward and wrapping around the back of the thigh, or along the inseam from groin to knee to remove excess skin. Patients with less laxity may qualify for a minimal-incision approach using only a groin incision.
  • Outer thigh incision — may require an incision extending from the groin around the hip and across the back; techniques place it where clothing conceals it, but incisions may be extensive.
  • Closure — deep sutures support the new contours and the skin is closed with sutures, adhesive tapes or clips. Cleveland Clinic describes removing fat, repositioning tissue, tightening nearby muscle and trimming excess skin.

The society says the smoother contour is apparent almost immediately, though initially obscured by swelling and bruising. It may be combined with a buttock or lower-body lift. So the questions are which of the four types, where the incision runs, and whether liposuction is combined.

How do you prepare for Thigh Lift?

The first step is confirming you are a suitable candidate. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons lists people whose weight is relatively stable, with excess soft tissue on the inner or outer thigh, without conditions that impair healing or raise surgical risk, non-smokers, with realistic goals and a commitment to a healthy lifestyle.

Cleveland Clinic is more specific: your goal weight maintained for a year with no plan to lose more, because further weight change loosens the tightened skin again. Conditions such as diabetes should be well managed.

Physical preparation is the same as for other incisional surgery.

  • Disclose conditions, medications and supplements; some may be adjusted because of bleeding risk.
  • Smoking affects wound healing, so you will be asked to stop for a period.
  • Complete pre-anesthesia tests and check whether they are included in the quote.
  • Plan for up to 10 days of resting your legs and at least six weeks in a compression garment (Cleveland Clinic) — arrange time, help at home and a ride home.

What does recovery after Thigh Lift involve?

Right after surgery, dressings are applied and small thin drains may be placed temporarily under the skin to remove fluid or blood (American Society of Plastic Surgeons). Cleveland Clinic says a compression garment is worn for at least six weeks, the legs are rested as much as possible for up to 10 days, and the first follow-up is after one week.

The course:

  • Early — the society stresses keeping the incisions free of excessive force, swelling, abrasion or motion while healing; this area is under tension with every step.
  • Around six weeks — most people resume daily activities, per Cleveland Clinic; your surgeon sets the return-to-work date.
  • Six months — swelling has gone completely and the final result is visible.

Call promptly — the society lists shortness of breath, chest pain or unusual heartbeats as signs that may need hospital care. Cleveland Clinic adds bleeding, fever or chills, an incision unusually sore to touch, leg swelling, pain not relieved by medication, and wounds not healing in the first couple of weeks. Leg swelling and breathlessness can signal a clot — do not wait.

When should you contact the clinic after Thigh Lift?

Most swelling and bruising settles over time. The following, however, are described as signs to contact the clinic straight away rather than waiting for a scheduled follow-up.

  • Fever — may indicate infection.
  • Pain that improves and then worsens again
  • Sudden swelling, especially on one side only
  • Bleeding that does not stop, or a wound that opens
  • Pain and swelling in one calf — the American Society of Plastic Surgeons lists deep vein thrombosis (DVT) among the risks of this group of operations.
  • Shortness of breath or chest pain — seek emergency care immediately.

ASPS notes that limited movement during recovery raises the risk of clots, so light walking is sometimes encouraged early. Ask the operating surgeon what is allowed and from when.

Ask for out-of-hours and holiday contact details before the operation. Emergency arrangements are something to confirm at consultation.

What are the risks of Thigh Lift?

Every operation carries risk. The following summarises risks published by bodies such as the American Society of Plastic Surgeons and Mayo Clinic. It does not predict the outcome for any particular clinic or individual. The risks that apply to you must be explained to you directly at consultation.

  • Anesthesia risks — reactions vary with the type of anesthesia used
  • Deep vein thrombosis (DVT), cardiac and pulmonary complications — a clot forming in a deep leg vein can travel to the lungs and become dangerous. ASPS lists this explicitly; the likelihood rises with longer operating times and reduced movement during recovery. Ask at consultation how your risk is assessed and what prevention is used — compression devices, early walking and, where indicated, medication.
  • Bleeding or hematoma — blood collecting at the surgical site
  • Infection — at the wound site
  • Poor wound healing — recovery slower than expected
  • Fat necrosis — death of some fat tissue
  • Skin loss or skin necrosis — where blood supply to the incision is insufficient
  • Persistent pain — pain that lasts longer than expected
  • Unfavorable scarring — any incision leaves a scar; the degree varies between individuals
  • Asymmetry or a suboptimal aesthetic result — further surgery may be needed to correct it
  • Numbness or changes in skin sensation — temporary or lasting
  • Fluid accumulation (seroma) — drainage or further treatment may be required
  • Skin discoloration and prolonged swelling — lasting longer than expected
  • Recurrent skin looseness — skin can loosen again over time
  • Possibility of revisional surgery — further surgery may be needed for correction

What most affects risk is the surgeon's qualification and experience, the facility, the anesthesia staff, and the arrangements for emergencies. Verifying these four at consultation is the most practical safety check available to a patient.

Ask directly how clot prevention is handled. Smoking, obesity, a history of clots and hormone medication are known to raise the risk — disclose them if they apply to you. Long-haul flights are relevant here: if you are travelling for treatment, ask how soon after surgery it is safe to fly.

During recovery, fever, worsening pain, sudden swelling, bleeding or wound separation — or pain and swelling in one calf, breathlessness or chest pain — should never be self-assessed. Contact the treating clinic and seek emergency care immediately. Confirm the contact route and hours before surgery, and if you are returning to another country, agree how follow-up will be handled.

What determines the cost of Thigh Lift in Seoul?

What sets the price of a thigh lift is above all the type — a mini lift with a small groin incision, an inner lift along the inseam, an outer lift wrapping around the hip to the back, or a spiral lift differ in incision length and operating time, and that is the price difference. Added to that are combined liposuction, anesthesia, an overnight stay, and whether the compression garment (six weeks or more) is included.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons says the total is made up of anesthesia, facility, tests, garments, prescriptions and the surgeon's fee, which depends on experience, technique and location. Treating both thighs and the hips together lengthens the operation, raising anesthesia and facility costs, so ask each clinic type, number of areas, incision length, anesthesia and stay in the same order.

Cosmetic surgery is not covered by national insurance and each clinic posts its own price. This site does not publish the listed clinic's prices; ranges published by bodies in other countries are on the guide page about non-covered pricing.

How do you verify a surgeon in Korea?

A thigh lift involves long incisions and closure that determines the result, so verifying the surgeon comes first. Look the surgeon up by name in the Korean plastic surgery society registries and check the clinic's specialists by field and registered operating rooms and anesthesiology in the HIRA registry.

Questions specific to a thigh lift:

  • Type and incision — mini, inner, outer or spiral, and exactly where the scar will run; ask to see it drawn.
  • Why that type — how the extent of laxity and the amount of fat were assessed.
  • Anesthesia and who provides it — sedation or general, and whether a dedicated anesthesia provider is present.
  • Clot prevention — it is leg surgery, so how deep-vein thrombosis is prevented and where to call if signs appear.
  • Consultation–surgery match — whether the doctor you consult is the one who operates, and whether the consent form names the same surgeon.
