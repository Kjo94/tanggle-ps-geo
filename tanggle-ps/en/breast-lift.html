How does Breast Lift (Mastopexy) differ from other lift procedures?

A breast lift begins with an incision around the areola, with further incisions extending downward as the degree of ptosis increases — the degree of sagging therefore determines the extent of scarring. The plan changes if augmentation is combined, and if you are planning to breastfeed, that should be raised at consultation.

How is Breast Lift (Mastopexy) performed?

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons defines a breast lift (mastopexy) as surgery that raises the breasts by removing excess skin and tightening the surrounding tissue. It lists pregnancy, breastfeeding, weight fluctuations, aging, gravity and heredity as causes of sagging. A lift does not significantly change breast size or round out the upper breast, so augmentation is considered for fullness and reduction for smaller breasts.

The steps, as the society describes them:

  • Anesthesia — intravenous sedation or general anesthesia. Cleveland Clinic says the operation usually lasts an hour or two.
  • Incision — three patterns — around the areola; around the areola and vertically down to the breast crease; or around the areola, vertically down and horizontally along the crease (inverted T / anchor). The choice depends on breast size and shape, areola size and position, degree of sagging, and skin elasticity and the amount of extra skin. Cleveland Clinic also describes a crescent incision along the upper areola.
  • Reshaping — underlying tissue is lifted and reshaped, and excess skin removed to compensate for lost elasticity.
  • Nipple and areola — repositioned to a more youthful height; enlarged areolas are reduced by excising skin at the perimeter if needed.
  • Closure — remaining skin is tightened as incisions are closed; layered internal sutures support the new shape, and the skin is closed with sutures, adhesive or tape.

Results are immediately visible, the society says. So the questions are which of the three incision patterns (which is the scar), whether the areola is reduced, and whether augmentation or reduction is combined.

How do you prepare for Breast Lift (Mastopexy)?

The first step is confirming you are a suitable candidate. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons lists people who are physically healthy with a stable weight, non-smokers, and doing it for themselves. Breast findings include a flatter, elongated or pendulous shape, nipples that fall below the breast crease when unsupported, downward-pointing nipples and areolas, stretched skin and enlarged areolas, or one breast lower than the other.

What the procedure does not do — the society states it does not significantly change size or round out the upper breast; a lift alone will not add volume.

Physical preparation, per Cleveland Clinic:

  • Have a mammogram.
  • Stop smoking and tobacco products.
  • You may be asked to stop medications that affect bleeding such as aspirin, NSAIDs and herbal supplements.
  • Blood work, a physical exam and a consent discussion.
  • Breastfeeding plans — Cleveland Clinic lists inability to breastfeed afterwards as a risk; raise future plans at consultation.

What does recovery after Breast Lift (Mastopexy) involve?

Right after surgery, dressings are applied and an elastic bandage or support bra is worn to minimize swelling and support the breasts. A small thin drain may be placed temporarily (American Society of Plastic Surgeons); Cleveland Clinic says drains are removed a few days later. Most people go home the same day.

The course, per Cleveland Clinic:

  • About a week — many return to desk work and gentle activities such as walking.
  • Up to six weeks — vigorous activity is restricted.
  • About eight weeks — a regular bra can usually be worn again.
  • Scars are permanent but fade a lot over time; results should last for several years.

Sensation — numbness or changed sensation of the breast skin, nipples or areolas can occur, per Cleveland Clinic. The society suggests asking about medication, when dressings and stitches come out, when to resume activity and exercise, and follow-up timing.

Call promptly for breast redness, heat, swelling or pain; bleeding, drainage, redness or swelling at the incision; chest pain; dizziness; fever above 100.5°F (38°C); severe pain unrelieved by medication; or nausea and vomiting (Cleveland Clinic).

When should you contact the clinic after Breast Lift (Mastopexy)?

Most swelling and bruising settles over time. The following, however, are described as signs to contact the clinic straight away rather than waiting for a scheduled follow-up.

  • Fever — may indicate infection.
  • Pain that improves and then worsens again
  • Sudden swelling, especially on one side only
  • Bleeding that does not stop, or a wound that opens
  • Pain and swelling in one calf — the American Society of Plastic Surgeons lists deep vein thrombosis (DVT) among the risks of this group of operations.
  • Shortness of breath or chest pain — seek emergency care immediately.

ASPS notes that limited movement during recovery raises the risk of clots, so light walking is sometimes encouraged early. Ask the operating surgeon what is allowed and from when.

Ask for out-of-hours and holiday contact details before the operation. Emergency arrangements are something to confirm at consultation.

What are the risks of Breast Lift (Mastopexy)?

Every operation carries risk. The following summarises risks published by bodies such as the American Society of Plastic Surgeons and Mayo Clinic. It does not predict the outcome for any particular clinic or individual. The risks that apply to you must be explained to you directly at consultation.

  • Anesthesia risks — reactions vary with the type of anesthesia used
  • Deep vein thrombosis (DVT), cardiac and pulmonary complications — a clot forming in a deep leg vein can travel to the lungs and become dangerous. ASPS lists this explicitly; the likelihood rises with longer operating times and reduced movement during recovery. Ask at consultation how your risk is assessed and what prevention is used — compression devices, early walking and, where indicated, medication.
  • Bleeding or hematoma — blood collecting at the surgical site
  • Infection — at the wound site
  • Poor wound healing — recovery slower than expected
  • Fat necrosis — death of some fat tissue
  • Skin loss or skin necrosis — where blood supply to the incision is insufficient
  • Persistent pain — pain that lasts longer than expected
  • Unfavorable scarring — any incision leaves a scar; the degree varies between individuals
  • Asymmetry or a suboptimal aesthetic result — further surgery may be needed to correct it
  • Numbness or changes in skin sensation — temporary or lasting
  • Fluid accumulation (seroma) — drainage or further treatment may be required
  • Skin discoloration and prolonged swelling — lasting longer than expected
  • Recurrent skin looseness — skin can loosen again over time
  • Possibility of revisional surgery — further surgery may be needed for correction

What most affects risk is the surgeon's qualification and experience, the facility, the anesthesia staff, and the arrangements for emergencies. Verifying these four at consultation is the most practical safety check available to a patient.

Ask directly how clot prevention is handled. Smoking, obesity, a history of clots and hormone medication are known to raise the risk — disclose them if they apply to you. Long-haul flights are relevant here: if you are travelling for treatment, ask how soon after surgery it is safe to fly.

During recovery, fever, worsening pain, sudden swelling, bleeding or wound separation — or pain and swelling in one calf, breathlessness or chest pain — should never be self-assessed. Contact the treating clinic and seek emergency care immediately. Confirm the contact route and hours before surgery, and if you are returning to another country, agree how follow-up will be handled.

What determines the cost of Breast Lift (Mastopexy) in Seoul?

What sets the price of a breast lift is the incision pattern (areola only · areola plus vertical · inverted T) and whether augmentation or reduction is combined. More sagging means a longer incision and operating time, and combining augmentation adds the implant as a separate material cost. Then come anesthesia, an overnight stay (most go home the same day) and whether the support bra and follow-up visits are included.

The American Society of Plastic Surgeons says the total is made up of anesthesia, facility, tests, garments, prescriptions and the surgeon's fee, which depends on experience, technique and location. Areola reduction and nipple repositioning are usually part of the operation rather than separate lines, but clinics differ, so ask incision pattern, combined surgery, implant, anesthesia and stay in the same order.

If augmentation is combined, ask for a quote that lists the implant separately. This site does not publish the listed clinic's prices; ranges published by bodies in other countries are on the guide page about non-covered pricing.

How do you verify a surgeon in Korea?

In a breast lift the choice of incision pattern and nipple position determines the result, so verifying the surgeon comes first. Look the surgeon up by name in the Korean plastic surgery society registries and check the clinic's specialists by field and registered operating rooms and anesthesiology in the HIRA registry.

Questions specific to a breast lift:

  • Incision pattern and reasoning — areola-only, vertical or inverted T, and how sagging and skin elasticity were assessed; ask to see the scar drawn.
  • Combined augmentation or reduction — if volume is the goal a lift alone is not enough; does the surgeon say so first.
  • Nipple and areola — where they will sit, whether the areola is reduced, and whether sensation change and possible inability to breastfeed are explained.
  • Anesthesia and who provides it — sedation or general, and whether a dedicated anesthesia provider is present.
  • Consultation–surgery match — whether the doctor you consult is the one who operates, and whether the consent form names the same surgeon.
