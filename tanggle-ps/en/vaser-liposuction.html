How is VASER Liposuction performed?

Liposuction follows this sequence: marking → anesthesia → infiltration → suction → closure. Marking, done while standing, directly affects the outcome.

  • Marking — the areas to treat and to preserve are marked while standing.
  • Anesthesia — chosen according to the extent of treatment.
  • Infiltration — fluid is introduced into the fat layer to assist suction and reduce bleeding.
  • Suction — a cannula is introduced through small incisions. Devices differ in how they loosen or emulsify fat before removal.
  • Closure — incisions are closed and a compression garment applied.

Treating a wide area in one session means longer anesthesia and a heavier recovery. Whether to stage areas, or to combine with a lift, is decided after examining skin quality and tissue volume.

How do you prepare for VASER Liposuction?

Preparation has two sides: confirming your medical condition and arranging your schedule. Both depend on individual circumstances, so the instructions you receive at consultation are what govern.

  • Existing conditions and medication — disclose all conditions, prescription medicines and supplements. Some medicines and supplements affect bleeding and may need adjusting.
  • Smoking and alcohol — both can affect wound healing and are usually restricted for a period.
  • Pre-operative tests — carried out for anesthesia and safety. Check whether these are included in the quoted price.
  • Schedule — allow generous time for recovery and arrange in advance who will help you and how you will travel home.
  • What to bring — compression garments, loose clothing and any items you are advised to prepare.

Ask for the pre-operative explanation in writing. Having the surgical scope, expected course, cost breakdown and revision terms on paper makes everything easier to verify later — this matters particularly if you are travelling from abroad.

What does recovery after VASER Liposuction involve?

Swelling and bruising peak soon after surgery and subside over time. The final contour can only be judged once swelling has resolved, so early appearance is not a reliable indication of the result.

  • Compression garment — duration and method vary with the area treated, and adherence affects how swelling resolves.
  • Return to activity — depends on intensity; confirm the criteria at consultation.
  • Follow-up — asymmetry or contour irregularity may change as healing progresses, so keep to the scheduled checks.

Contact the treating clinic immediately if you notice fever, unusual pain or asymmetry. Because liposuction addresses a wide area beneath the skin, aftercare and follow-up influence the result.

What are the risks of VASER Liposuction?

Every operation carries risk. The following summarises risks published by bodies such as the American Society of Plastic Surgeons and Mayo Clinic. It does not predict the outcome for any particular clinic or individual. The risks that apply to you must be explained to you directly at consultation.

  • Anesthesia risks
  • Bleeding or hematoma — blood collecting at the surgical site
  • Infection
  • Delayed wound healing
  • Scarring — any incision leaves a scar; the degree varies
  • Asymmetry or contour irregularity — revision surgery may be needed
  • Changes in sensation — temporary or lasting
  • Fluid collection — drainage or further treatment may be required

What most affects risk is the surgeon's qualification and experience, the facility, the anesthesia staff, and the arrangements for emergencies. Verifying these four at consultation is the most practical safety check available to a patient.

During recovery, fever, worsening pain, sudden swelling, bleeding or wound separation should never be self-assessed — contact the treating clinic immediately. Confirm the contact route and hours before surgery, and if you are returning to another country, agree how follow-up will be handled.

What determines the cost of VASER Liposuction in Seoul?

Two clinics may use the same procedure name for a different actual surgical scope, which changes the price. Comparing totals hides what is included, so compare line by line.

  • Surgical fee — what it covers
  • Anesthesia — method, and whether staffing is a separate line
  • Pre-operative tests — included or separate
  • Materials — implants, sutures, compression garments
  • Inpatient or recovery room — where same-day discharge does not apply
  • Follow-up visits — how many are included, and what happens after
  • VAT — cosmetic procedures are taxable in Korea

In Korea, cosmetic surgery is non-covered care — it falls outside national health insurance. Clinics set their own prices for non-covered items and are required to publish them, which is why the same procedure name carries different prices between clinics. There is therefore no such thing as a standard price. Published non-covered pricing can be checked through the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service (HIRA).

Advertised entry prices usually apply only under specific conditions. Compare a written quotation issued after your own examination, and confirm what triggers additional cost and how revision is handled. Where a dispute arises over cost or the explanation given, the Korea Consumer Agency provides consultation and dispute resolution procedures.

How do you verify a surgeon in Korea?

In Korea, 'board-certified plastic surgeon' and 'doctor practising plastic surgery' are not the same thing. Certification follows a defined training programme; a clinic's listed specialty alone does not tell you whether the operating doctor holds it.

  • Board certification — verifiable through the Korean Society of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgeons
  • Clinic registration — licensing and registered specialties can be checked through HIRA
  • Who operates — whether the doctor who consults is the one who operates on the day
  • Facility and staffing — whether anesthesia staff are present and what emergency arrangements exist
  • Experience with the specific procedure

Checking both sources together shows whether advertising matches the registered record. Review scores alone are a weak basis, because a rating does not measure surgical safety.

Situations that push you to decide quickly are themselves worth pausing over — discounts valid only if you sign the same day, a consulting doctor who differs from the operating surgeon without explanation, or refusal to issue a written quotation. Surgery is difficult to reverse; taking more than a day to decide is the safer course. If you are travelling for surgery, also confirm how complications would be managed after you return home.
